Statistical analysis — R & SPSS outputs
R — linear regression: predictors of attendance rate
lm() · DV: attendance rate · n = 133 · run in R 4.3
Regression coefficients: enrollment type FT positive, IEP negative, DLL slight negative, subsidy positive.

R² = 0.38 · Adj. R² = 0.36 · F(4,128) = 19.6, p < .001

What this means in plain language: This analysis asked — what factors are actually associated with whether a child shows up? The bars show the direction and strength of each factor. Green bars push attendance up; red bars pull it down. Children enrolled full-time attend significantly more often than part-time children. Having an IEP or IFSP is associated with slightly lower attendance — likely reflecting the additional coordination and appointment burden on families. The model explains about 38% of the variation in attendance rates, which is a solid fit for real-world program data.

SPSS — one-way ANOVA: CLASS ES by age group
DV: CLASS Emotional Support · IV: age group (3–4 yr vs 4–5 yr) · Cronbach's α = 0.86
CLASS Emotional Support scores compared between 3-4 year and 4-5 year age groups.

F(1,5) = 4.82, p = .08 · η² = 0.18 · Tukey HSD applied

What this means in plain language: This test asked — do teachers score differently on Emotional Support depending on whether they're working with 3–4 year olds versus 4–5 year olds? The result (p = .08) is close to significant but doesn't cross the standard threshold. In practical terms: there's a trend suggesting 4–5 year old classrooms score slightly higher on Emotional Support, but the sample is too small (7 classrooms) to say this with confidence. Worth watching in a larger dataset. Cronbach's α = 0.86 confirms the CLASS items are measuring the same underlying thing consistently.

Analytical findings & equity flags
What the data shows
Synthesized from R regression, SPSS ANOVA, and ECERS-3 subscale analysis
✓
Emotional Support scores are high program-wide (M = 6.1/7) and do not track with ECERS composite — confirming that low composite scores reflect structural constraints, not teacher quality or program culture.
✓
Full-time enrollment is the strongest positive predictor of attendance (β = +0.091, p < .001). Conversion outreach to part-time families is a high-leverage QI target.
!
Handwashing compliance is inconsistent and should not be treated as a program-wide failure. CR03 and CR06 score lowest — both have structural and staffing constraints that explain the pattern. Targeted coaching, not broad PD, is indicated.
▲
CR06 is a priority equity flag. Highest IEP load (4 children), lowest attendance (79.8%), vacant co-teacher since September. This is a resource allocation issue, not a teacher performance issue.
